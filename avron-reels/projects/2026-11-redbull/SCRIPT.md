# Red Bull Media Flywheel: 3:00 master script (v2, fact-checked)

- **Format:** 9:16, 1080×1920, 30 fps, English VO, about 430 words (about 2.4 words a second, so it leaves room for the dead-air beat at 0:25).
- **Goal:** followers. **Library prompt:** #9 Script method (hook, value, solution, CTA).
- **Look:** the user's palette (one reel only, like Palantir) with AVRON's pro motion rules. See `DESIGN.md`.
- Every number traces to `research/FACTS.md`. Lines marked † changed from the pasted script because the fact was wrong or had no source.

## VO (paste-ready, one block)

> **HOOK (0:00–0:10)**
> What if I told you Red Bull doesn't give a damn about selling energy drinks?
>
> **VALUE (0:10–1:55)**
> Coca-Cola spent about five billion dollars on advertising last year, buying thirty seconds at a time. † Red Bull still runs ads too. But most of its money goes somewhere else, and it built a twelve-billion-dollar business doing it. †
>
> Red Bull is an extreme sports production house that pays for itself by selling aluminum cans. †
>
> Let's talk numbers. Red Bull doesn't publish its cost per can. Industry estimates put the drink, the can and the packaging at around fifteen cents. † You pay about three fifty at a store in the US. That's a massive margin.
>
> Instead of pocketing it, Red Bull spends an estimated quarter to a third of its revenue on marketing. † They don't sponsor Formula One cars. They own Red Bull Racing. They don't buy stadium ads. They own the teams: Leipzig, Salzburg, New York. † Every event makes footage, the footage earns attention for free, attention sells cans, and the cans pay for the next event.
>
> On October fourteenth, 2012, Felix Baumgartner stepped out of a capsule about a hundred and twenty-eight thousand feet up, at the edge of space, and jumped. YouTube said the live stream peaked above eight million concurrent streams. Its record at the time. †
>
> Red Bull paid no broadcast rights fee to anyone, because it owned the event, the footage and the media network. † The project wasn't free. Outside estimates put it at thirty to fifty million dollars. † Analysts valued the exposure in the hundreds of millions. †
>
> There was no product pitch during the broadcast. It didn't need one. † For hours, the logo sat on the capsule, the suit and the stream, next to the edge of human capability. That's the feeling you buy with every can. †
>
> **SOLUTION (2:15–2:40)**
> This is the content-first business model. Step one: build a media engine people actually want to watch. Step two: fund it with a high-margin physical product. Step three: turn customers from buyers into believers.
>
> **CTA (2:40–3:00)**
> Don't build ads for your product. Build a media brand your audience loves, and let the product be the entry ticket. Follow for the money behind the world's biggest brands.

## What changed from the pasted script
- **Removed "ignoring traditional advertising entirely".** Red Bull ran a Super Bowl spot in 2025. The paradox now rests on where most of the money goes.
- **"$12 billion enterprise"** is now "a twelve-billion-dollar business" (2024 sales €11.2 bn), not a valuation.
- **"Up to 30%"** is now "an estimated quarter to a third", because Red Bull doesn't publish it and sources range from 25% to 33%.
- **15 cents** stays, but it's labelled as an industry estimate on screen and in the VO.
- **"$50 million in ad buys" is cut.** I found no source. The scorecard now shows the $0 rights fee, the $30–50 m project-cost estimate and the earned-media estimate.
- **"Eight million people watched"** is now "peaked above eight million concurrent streams", which is what YouTube reported.
- **"Didn't sell a single can"** is now "no product pitch". Nobody can verify the first version.
- **The flywheel line was added to the VO** so the 0:50–1:15 diagram has words under it.
- no-ai-slop pass: the binary contrast "Red Bull is not a beverage company. It's…" became one direct sentence (the hook card still says it, because that's the on-screen hook you asked for). The kicker "holding a piece of extreme performance in your hand" became a concrete line about where the logo sat. I kept "What if I told you" and "doesn't give a damn" because that's your voice. If you want a harder open, use: "Red Bull sold 12.7 billion cans last year. The cans aren't the business."
