## 1. Method and limits

What was observed directly

- YouTube channel listings (titles, view counts, durations, upload dates) for Etymology Nerd, India in Pixels, Geo Study, TheGeoExplorer, Geo All Day, RobWords, Kurzgesagt, Johnny Harris, Dhruv Rathee, The Better India, Geography Now and Rekhta Dictionary, pulled with yt-dlp on 6 November 2026. Where a teardown cites a title, duration, view count or upload date, it comes from this.
- YouTube auto-captions and top public comments for a set of reference videos (Geo Study, TheGeoExplorer, Map Men, Geography Now, Kurzgesagt, Johnny Harris, The Better India, Geo All Day, India in Pixels, Etymology Nerd, RobWords), where the fetch succeeded. The teardowns say which fields came from captions. Where a fetch failed, the field reads "n/o".
- A Geo Study Telegram channel page (post captions, hashtags, view counts).
- Third-party tracker pages (HypeAuditor, iQfluence, Qoruz, CreatorDB, vidIQ, socialcounts) for follower counts. Each number in the table carries its source and date.

What was described secondhand

- Instagram itself. The Instagram channel in Agent Reach returned an upstream error and no login was attempted; direct fetches of instagram.com profiles returned HTTP 403/429. Every Instagram reel in this document is therefore described from press profiles, interviews, podcast transcripts, tracker pages and the creator's YouTube mirror. No Instagram reel was watched. Where a teardown field could only be known by watching the Instagram reel (colour grade, typography, SFX), it reads "n/o" or is explicitly marked "described from press".
- Reddit. Three Reddit mirrors returned 403/503 during the research window, so public comment quotes come from YouTube comment threads, Tildes, and press articles that quote social media comments, each with a URL.

Follower counts are snapshots from different tools on different dates and are not comparable to each other. Two discrepancies are flagged in the table (TheGeoExplorer, India in Pixels). Nothing in this document describes a reel that was not observed without saying so.

### 5.2 Recognisable identity brief

Eight things this page will do that no account in the niche currently does:

1. Every reel carries its source on screen, in frame, for the full duration of the claim. Not in the caption.
2. Every reel has one declared replay device, chosen before the storyboard: a hidden state, a counter to verify, a seamless loop, or an ending that returns to the opening with one word changed.
3. Scripts move. A word crosses scripts on screen (Nasta'liq to Devanagari to Tamil to Roman) as its story moves, in a type system designed to hold all scheduled scripts at phone size.
4. A sonic logo and a small, fixed SFX kit on every reel: a state-lands click, a word-morph sweep, a counter tick. No stock whoosh.
5. Three regions minimum per reel, or none. No map is shown with fewer than all states and union territories drawn; the Survey of India outline only.
6. A Northeast and small-languages slot on a schedule, with native-speaker voices for the regional words, not a Hindi voice reading Mizo.
7. A standing corrections series. Wrong claims get a card, the fix and the source, within a week.
8. A weekly community loop: Friday prompt, Sunday map of the replies, credited.

Five things we will not do:

1. Copy the fast-talking face format (Etymology Nerd, RobWords, Karnatac). The page is faceless; speed-as-replay is not ours.
2. Use "Through Map Animation", "facts that will blow your mind", "wait for it", or any existing catchphrase ("We're the men and here's the map").
3. Borrow Kurzgesagt's flat bird world, Harris's map-as-camera push, or Geo Study's notebook palette. No stock footage over a map.
4. Run geopolitics ("why X hasn't been invaded"), exam prep, or mythology retellings. Words, names, scripts and maps of how India speaks only.
5. Post a claim on a language's age, origin or "mother" status, or a word map built from a prefix search. If it cannot be sourced to a dictionary or a peer-reviewed etymology, it does not ship.

| Caste and community words | Not observed directly; slang pages avoid it | n/a | Slang reels exclude caste slurs and community nicknames entirely; a word list is reviewed by a second person before scripting |
| Filming people for language content | Varanasi creator confronting tourists (Jan 2026) | "0 Civic Sense. 0 Regards for their privacy." | The page is faceless and never films strangers; community replies are quoted with consent and credited by handle only |

Working rule set, in order:

1. Source first. A claim with no dictionary, corpus or peer-reviewed etymology does not enter the script.
2. Local reader second. Any regional word, place name or script detail is checked with a native speaker or a regional-language source before the storyboard.
3. Many regions, no centre. Three or more regions per reel; the object leads, not a language.
4. Map asset locked. One Survey of India outline, all states and UTs, dated boundaries, no exceptions.
5. No rankings. No "oldest", "mother", "purest", "real" language claims.
6. Corrections are content. A standing series with the fix and the source, within a week.
7. Faceless and consent-based. No filmed strangers; community quotes credited and consented.
