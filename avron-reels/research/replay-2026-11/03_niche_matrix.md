## 3. The ten research questions

### Q1. What do Indians consume repeatedly?

- [FACT] 588 million Indians, 61% of the internet population, consumed short video in 2025; rural users marginally outnumber urban. (IAMAI-Kantar, Internet in India 2025, via Business Standard.)
- [FACT] In Meta's commissioned Ipsos survey of 3,500+ people across 33 centres, 95% said they watch Reels every day, and 97% watch short video at least once a day. (Meta newsroom, Sept 2025.)
- [FACT] Redseer found 84% of 1,124 respondents believe they spend more time on short video than they planned. (Redseer SFV report via afaqs.)
- [FACT] Comedy leads content consumption at 45%, followed by movies and daily soaps at 35% and fashion at 33% (BCG, May 2025, 1,900+ consumers).
- [FACT] Instagram's India 2025 Year-in-Review lists cricket, nostalgia (Wake Up Sid, Rockstar songs, Yeh Jawaani Hai Deewani), the lo-fi digicam aesthetic, Shark Tank clips and the Vishal Mega Mart meme as what kept India scrolling.
- [FACT] Instagram added a Reels Watch History in October 2025 so people can refind reels they watched but did not like, save or share.
- [INTERPRETATION] The Watch History launch is Instagram admitting that "I want to see that again" is a common, unmet need. Rewatching is a real behaviour, not just a ranking trick.
- [INTERPRETATION] Comedy and film dominate what people say they watch, but both are supply-saturated and clip-dependent. The replay behaviour that matters for a faceless page is the quieter kind: loops, maps you need two passes to read, word reveals you replay to check.
- [HYPOTHESIS] In India, repeat viewing clusters around identity content (my city, my language, my childhood) more than around pure information. Nostalgia's presence in the official year-in-review supports this but does not prove it.

### Q2. Which niches get strong organic sharing?

- [FACT] Mosseri (Jan 2025) named watch time, likes and sends as the top three ranking signals, and in 2024 said "sends per reach correlate more, in my experience, with overall reach than anything else." (Social Media Today; Social Samosa.)
- [FACT] Reels are reshared more than 4.5 billion times a day globally, and messaging is now the most popular way to share photos and videos on Instagram. (Meta newsroom, Sept 2025.)
- [FACT] In Instagram's 2024 Trend Talk (US, UK, Brazil, India, South Korea), Gen Z's top ways of interacting included sending reels or memes in DMs. Country split not published.
- [FACT] Comscore H1 2025: Media and Entertainment accounts for 47% of social actions in India, influencers 21%, Sports 20%.
- [INTERPRETATION] The reels Indians send are the ones that say something about the recipient: "this is you", "this is us", "this is our city". Regional identity, language, nostalgia, cricket and relatable comedy all carry that property. Pure information (science, world history) gets saved more than sent.
- [HYPOTHESIS] A "what do you call this in your language" reel is sent to a friend from another state at a higher rate than almost any other faceless format, because the send is the joke. Untested.

### Q3. Where do repeat viewers (followers who come back) form?

- [FACT] Reels viewing in India "spans life stages, from college students to middle-aged professionals" and Meta describes Reels as "as habitual as breathing." (afaqs, Five years of Reels.)
- [FACT] More than 60% of content on Instagram and Facebook is AI-recommended rather than from followed accounts. (afaqs, citing Meta.)
- [INTERPRETATION] Because most views are recommended, a follow has to be earned by a format the viewer wants more of, not by a topic. Series formats (same frame, new fact) build repeat viewers; one-off virals do not.
- [INTERPRETATION] Niches with a built-in "next one" (one word a day, one state a day, one train a day) convert follows better than niches whose next reel is unpredictable.

### Q4. Which niches have high save potential?

- [FACT] Socialinsider's 2025 benchmarks (Jan 2023 to Dec 2024) found carousels get the most saves across brand sizes; an earlier study found reels get the most saves for the smallest profiles.
- [FACT] Vendor guidance (Kontentino) says educational content, tutorials and how-to guides typically generate the highest save rates, with recipes and infographics close behind. This is practitioner consensus, not a dataset.
- [INTERPRETATION] Saves favour reference value: travel lists, recipes, maps, word lists, health. Of these, maps and words also replay well; travel and health do not.
- [INTERPRETATION] Saves are the weakest of the three signals for reach (Mosseri did not name them in his 2025 top three), so saveability is weighted lower than sends and replay in section 5.

### Q5. Which niches produce meaningful comments?

- [FACT] The Social Samosa write-up of the 2025 year-in-review notes the RCB chant "Ee Sala Cup Namdu" and Veer Pahariya's "langdi" step as the year's comment-driving memes; both are tribal (team, fandom).
- [FACT] India in Pixels' most cited maps are "terms equivalent to Bhaiyya across states" and vegetarianism by state (Hindustan Times, Republic World). Both invite "in my state we say..." replies.
- [INTERPRETATION] Comments in India are strongest where a viewer can add a data point about themselves: their state, their language, their school, their team. Language and maps are the two faceless niches where every viewer has one.
- [HYPOTHESIS] Correction comments ("actually in Telangana we say...") are a feature, not a bug: they are long, specific and keep the reel in distribution. This is a working assumption for the winner's comment strategy.

### Q6. Which niches create belonging?

- [FACT] 98% of Indian internet users access Indic-language content; Tamil, Telugu and Malayalam are the most popular languages for digital content; 57% of urban users prefer regional-language content. (IAMAI-Kantar 2024.)
- [FACT] Tier 2+ users account for about 42% of short-form engagement and about 63% of them prefer regional-language content. (Redseer data via ShareChat/Moj MMA deck.)
- [FACT] Meta's Nov 2025 House of Instagram event announced AI dubbing of reels into Marathi, Bengali, Telugu, Kannada and Tamil, plus Devanagari and Bengali-Assamese fonts.
- [FACT] Regional-language tracks in Punjabi, Haryanvi, Marathi, Tamil and Bhojpuri were among Instagram India's biggest music trends in 2024.
- [INTERPRETATION] Belonging in India is linguistic and regional before it is topical. A page that lets a Malayali, a Punjabi and a Bihari each see themselves in the same reel builds a bigger community than one that serves a single identity.
- [INTERPRETATION] Meta investing in regional dubbing and fonts means the platform will reward multilingual reels mechanically, not just culturally.

### Q7. Which niches sustain daily publishing?

- [FACT] India has 2 to 2.5 million creators with over 1,000 followers (BCG) or 3.5 to 4.5 million by Kofluence's wider count; only 8 to 10% monetise.
- [INTERPRETATION] Supply of raw material: languages (22 scheduled, hundreds of dialects, thousands of place names, loanwords in every direction), maps (thousands of datasets), trains (68,000 km of track and a story per station) and food (every district) are effectively infinite. Nostalgia is finite and clip-dependent; cricket is calendar-dependent; original animation is labour-bound.
- [INTERPRETATION] A Remotion pipeline turns data into reels at a fixed cost per reel once the template exists. Niches whose reels are "one dataset, one template" (words, maps, infrastructure stats) are the only ones that sustain a daily cadence for a one-person team.

### Q8. Where is creative competition weak?

- [FACT] Indian geography on Instagram is served mostly by exam-prep pages: Geo All Day 1.3M, Geo Study 434K, Geography Made Easy 269K, Geography Knowledge Academy 300K (HypeAuditor). The Geoexplorer (908K) is the main non-exam maps-and-stories page.
- [FACT] Indian psychology is led by face-led quote and self-help accounts: Ashish Bagrecha 2.4M, Antara Banerjee 0.6M, Upen Verma 0.5M (Yoloco), plus a long tail of one-fact-per-post pages that faceless-page guides recommend as a starter format.
- [FACT] Indian railways fan pages are many and small: All Indian Railway 109K, Indian Railways (indian_railways__) 68K, Indian Rail Insta 74K, iRailways 16K; the rest are under 3K.
- [FACT] Nostalgia: The90sIndia has about 665K; RetroBollywood claims 1.9M followers and 1.6 billion views (sponsored piece, self-reported).
- [FACT] Memes: RVCJ Media alone is around 13M; meme page lists run to 25+ pages above 1M.
- [FACT] Language: no Indian etymology or "how India speaks" page surfaced in any search. The closest are Urdu poetry pages (Ishq Urdu 311K, WordsinUrdu), a Bengaluru slang page (Slangaluru), and one Kannada-learning creator at 355K. Internationally, @etymologynerd has 1.6M Instagram followers, which proves the format.
- [FACT] YouTube search surfaced a cluster of AI-generated "Ghibli style 90s Indian nostalgia" channels with views in the hundreds to low thousands.
- [INTERPRETATION] Competition is weakest in language, trains and infrastructure, cities and design. It is strongest in memes, comedy, health, psychology quotes and personal finance. Nostalgia and history are being flooded with AI-generated faceless content, which lowers the bar but also poisons the well: viewers are learning to scroll past that look.

### Q9. Which niches stay relevant for five years?

- [FACT] Ormax's 2026 OTT report shows micro dramas (+50%), K-dramas (+48%) and anime (+32%) as the fastest-growing formats; Google's 2025 Year in Search was led by IPL, Gemini, Asia Cup, the Champions Trophy, Kabaddi, Maha Kumbh and the women's World Cup.
- [FACT] Devotional music streams on Amazon Music India roughly tripled between 2021 and 2025; the 1992 Hanuman Chalisa video passed 5 billion YouTube views in Nov 2025 (Forbes India).
- [INTERPRETATION] Formats and memes rotate yearly. Identity, language, geography, food, faith and cricket do not. The winner should sit on one of those, with formats refreshed on top.
- [INTERPRETATION] AI dubbing and translation will flatten the language barrier for face-led creators over five years. A page about language itself benefits from that shift (more cross-language viewers) instead of being threatened by it.

### Q10. Where is the gap between demand and quality widest?

- [FACT] The lo-fi digicam aesthetic and "fake wedding" parties were among 2025's trends: audiences are rewarding texture and craft, not polish for its own sake (Instagram Year-in-Review 2025).
- [FACT] Faceless-page guides, Fiverr gigs and Gumroad kits for "AI history reels" and "psychology facts reels" are abundant (search results in section 8), which signals a flood of templated, low-craft supply in those niches.
- [FACT] The owner's own analyst found the AVRON page looked "low effort" and "made with Claude" (project memory). That is the quality bar the new page must clear on day one.
- [INTERPRETATION] The widest gap is in language and maps: high demand (identity, sharing, comments), near-zero Indian supply done with motion-design craft, and the cheap AI-image look does not fit the format (type and maps are drawn, not generated). History and psychology have demand but the gap is being filled badly by hundreds of pages; a new entrant competes against the viewer's fatigue, not against the leaders.

Gap in evidence: no Reddit threads were retrievable; no India-specific measure of rewatch or save rates by category exists in public sources. Q1, Q3 and Q5 therefore lean on interpretation.

## 5. Weighted scoring model

### 5.1 Criteria and weights

| Code | Criterion | Weight | Why this weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| SH | Shareability (sends per reach) | 12 | Mosseri: sends correlate with reach more than anything else; sends are how non-followers find the page |
| RP | Replay potential | 12 | The brief's premise. Views and watch time both include replays, so a replayed reel earns more of the top-ranked signal |
| RT | Retention potential | 10 | Watch time is Mosseri's first-named signal for 2025 |
| PF | Production feasibility (faceless, no clips, Remotion-native) | 9 | A niche the owner cannot make daily, at quality, is not a niche |
| CS | Content supply | 8 | Daily publishing for years needs a near-infinite well |
| CD | Creative differentiation | 8 | The analyst's "looks made with Claude" verdict on AVRON; the new page must look like nothing else |
| SV | Saveability | 7 | Saves signal value but were not in Mosseri's 2025 top three |
| EM | Emotional appeal | 7 | Emotion drives sends and comments, but it is already partly counted in SH and CM |
| FC | Follow conversion | 6 | Important for compounding, but driven more by format consistency than by niche |
| CO | Competition (10 = weakest competition) | 6 | Weak competition helps, but strong competition also proves demand; mid weight |
| AU | Audience size | 5 | Every niche here has tens of millions of Indian viewers; size rarely decides |
| LG | Longevity (5-year relevance) | 5 | Most niches pass; it mainly penalises trend-bound ones |
| CM | Community potential | 5 | Valuable, but partly captured by SH and EM; kept separate so identity niches get credit |

Weights sum to 100. The weighted score is the sum of score times weight, divided by 100, so it reads on the same 1 to 10 scale.

### 5.3 What the ranking is sensitive to

- If replay weight drops from 12 to 6, maps and language still lead; puzzles fall out of the top ten. The winner does not depend on the replay premise being right.
- If production feasibility is set to zero (an owner willing to licence clips and hire actors), nostalgia (6.68 to about 7.1) and cricket rise but still trail the top four. The top of the table is not an artefact of the faceless constraint; it is reinforced by it.
- If audience size is doubled to 10, food overtakes trains and sits third. Food is the strongest "safe" alternative if the owner wants mass reach over differentiation.
- Scores of 2 to 3 on competition for memes, comedy, health, psychology and finance reflect saturation evidence in Q8; raising them to 6 lifts psychology to about 6.9, still below the top five.

## 6. Constraints applied

The page starts faceless and graphics-and-footage based: a Remotion motion-graphics pipeline, ElevenLabs voice, stock and free-licence footage, self-shot footage, no celebrity likenesses, no copyrighted film or TV clips without licence.

How the constraints shaped the matrix:

- Niches that need film, TV, match or music clips were scored 2 to 3 on production feasibility: Bollywood, regional cinema, cricket, music, nostalgia (when clip-based). These can be entered later with licensing or an illustrated approach, but not on day one.
- Niches that need a performer (comedy, micro-drama, lifestyle) were scored 3 to 4 unless the format can be fully animated.
- Niches where replay mechanisms fit the material were scored 8 to 10 on replay: loops (trains, animation, puzzles), hidden detail (maps, words), ending-reframes-opening (word origins, how-it's-made, puzzles).
- The owner's existing look (AVRON's clean product-UI) is not reused. The new page needs its own system, and the winner should be chosen partly because type and maps are the materials motion design does best.
- Sensitivity constraints: mythology, spirituality and language all touch identity politics. Language is the least volatile of the three if the page keeps a strict rule of sourcing every claim and naming many regions per reel.

## 7. Recommendation

### 7.1 Winner: Language and wordplay, "how India speaks"

Definition: the page explains India through its words. Word origins (why "chai" and "tea" are the same word on two trade routes), "what do you call it" maps (one object, twelve names, revealed one state per beat), place-name meanings (why so many cities end in -pur, -abad, -ur, -nagar), scripts (why Telugu letters are round), slang by city, loanwords in every direction (bungalow, loot, shampoo, catamaran went out; chair, station, paper came in), names and what they mean, and the words Indians share across languages without knowing it.

Why it wins:

1. Sends. The send is the joke: "this is what we call it" goes to the one friend from another state. Regional identity scored 9 on shareability and this niche inherits that while adding reference value.
2. Replay. A map that reveals twelve words in twelve seconds is watched twice: once to find your state, once to read the rest. Word-origin reels that end by reframing the first word ("so when you say 'namaste' you are also saying...") naturally loop.
3. Comments. Every viewer can add a data point, and correction comments are long and specific. India in Pixels' most cited maps are word maps (Q5).
4. Belonging across regions, not within one. The page can be the only thing a Malayali, a Punjabi and a Bihari follow together. Meta's new regional dubbing and fonts reward this mechanically (Q6).
5. Supply. Thousands of words, names and scripts, each one a reel; one dataset-plus-template can generate a daily series.
6. Competition. No quality Indian page exists in this niche; the international proof is @etymologynerd at 1.6M Instagram followers, and India has a far better story to tell (Q8).
7. Feasibility. Type and maps are what Remotion does best. No clips, no faces, no celebrities, no licences.
8. Longevity. Words do not expire; the dubbing wave makes cross-language curiosity bigger, not smaller (Q9).

Who the audience is: 18 to 34 first (77% of Indian Instagram), skewed to English-metro and Tier 2 graduates, bilingual, proud of where they are from, sending reels to friends from other states. Second ring: 35+ via WhatsApp forwards, because a "what we call it" map is the kind of thing a parent forwards to a family group. Diaspora (Punjabi, Tamil, Malayali, Gujarati) is a natural third ring.

The biggest risk: language politics and accuracy. A wrong etymology in Tamil, or a map that leaves out the North-East, will be caught and quoted. Hindi-first framing reads as imposition in the South. Mitigations: every claim sourced to a dictionary or peer-reviewed etymology before it ships; at least three regions named per reel or none; North-East and Kashmiri, Konkani, Tulu and tribal languages included on a schedule, not as an afterthought; a visible corrections habit in comments. Second risk: the niche can feel "educational" and cold. Mitigation: lead every reel with a human moment (a fight over what to call a snack) before the fact.

A sample week of reels (all faceless, Remotion type and maps, ElevenLabs VO in Hinglish with regional words spoken by native-speaker voices where available):

### 7.2 Alternative: Geography and maps of India

Why it is second: demand is proven (Geo All Day 1.3M, The Geoexplorer 908K, Geo Study 434K, a long tail of 100K to 300K pages), supply is infinite, production fits Remotion exactly, and the hidden-detail replay mechanism (find your state) is identical to the winner's. It loses to language on competition (the field is crowded with exam-prep pages) and on emotional appeal (a river map is interesting; a word map is personal). It is also the easiest niche to fold into the winner later: the same map engine serves both.

Audience: 18 to 30, exam-adjacent (UPSC, SSC) but not only; Tier 2 and 3 heavy; male-skewed like the platform. Biggest risk: being read as another exam-prep page, which caps follows and brand value, and border-depiction errors, which draw legal and political heat (always use the Survey of India outline).

Sample week: Mon "India vs Europe to scale"; Tue "the river that changed course in 1787"; Wed "every state's highest point in one climb"; Thu "the district nobody can name" (hidden-detail loop); Fri "which state are you: comment your district"; Sat "monsoon in 30 seconds" (seamless loop); Sun community map of comments.

Accounts to study: @geoallday (1.3M, HypeAuditor), @thegeoexplorerr (908K), @geostudy.in (434K), @geographymadeeasyy (269K), @geography_knowledge_academy_ (301K), @indian.maps (39K), @geographics.in, @thinkaboutmaps (195K, international), @very_useful_maps (about 100K, international), @braziliangeopoliticalmaps (117K, international).

### 7.3 Experimental: Puzzles and hidden-detail loops, India-flavoured

Why it is the experiment: it scores 10 on replay and 9 on feasibility but 4 on follow conversion and 5 on emotion. It is the purest test of the brief's thesis that people rewatch on purpose. If a 12-second "find the five errors in this map of Mumbai" reel gets two to three plays per reach, the mechanism is proven and can be lifted into the winner's formats. If it does not, the page loses a weekly slot, nothing more.

Audience: 16 to 28, broad, cross-language because puzzles need little text. Biggest risk: it becomes a gimmick page; puzzle reels are easy to copy and hard to brand. Run it as a Thursday slot inside the winner for eight weeks, then decide.

Sample week (if run standalone): Mon "which of these 12 words is not Sanskrit" (answer hidden in frame one); Tue "spot the fake state" on a map; Wed "one of these train names is invented"; Thu "find the English word in this Tamil sentence"; Fri "three city skylines, one is upside down"; Sat seamless loop with a single changed frame; Sun answers and leaderboard of first correct comments.

Accounts to study: Indian puzzle and illusion content is mostly republished by news sites (Inkl, Rojgarlive) rather than pages, which itself is a gap. Study @terriblemaps (over 2M across platforms; Instagram handle to verify) for map humour, @historyinmemes (about 2.1M) for how a "wait, look again" format builds a page, @very_useful_maps for one-frame map jokes, and @geoallday for geography quiz formats. Add the Indian meme leaders (@rvcjinsta, about 13M) for comment-bait mechanics.
