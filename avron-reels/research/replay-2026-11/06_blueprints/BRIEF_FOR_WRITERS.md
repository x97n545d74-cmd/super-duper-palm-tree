# Blueprint writers' brief (Module 6)

Shared instructions for the three agents writing Bolchaal's 30 reel blueprints. Read this, then the files listed under "Read first". Write only your assigned blueprints.

## The page
**Bolchaal**: a faceless Indian Instagram page about how India speaks. Word origins, "what do you call it" maps revealed state by state, place-name meanings, scripts, city slang, loanwords in both directions, Indian English. Built so people voluntarily rewatch (the Replay Principle). Motion graphics in Remotion, ElevenLabs VO (Indian-English newsroom accent, warm, 150 to 165 wpm), free-licence footage only, no faces, no film clips, no celebrity likenesses. 1080x1920, 30 fps.

## Non-negotiables
1. **Every language fact is checked.** Before you write a blueprint, WebSearch the etymology or usage claim against the sourcing policy in 05 (Etymonline/OED/Merriam-Webster for English; Hobson-Jobson for Indian-English loanwords; Rekhta/Platts/McGregor for Hindi-Urdu; Tamil Lexicon/DEDR for Dravidian; Census 2011 for speaker numbers; PLSI for small languages). Cite the source with a URL in the blueprint's claim list. Give each claim the confidence grade from 05. If a claim can't be verified, change the word or the reel. Never fabricate an etymology, a regional word, or a speaker count. Folk etymologies may appear only when labelled as folk etymology.
2. **Regional words for "what do you call it" reels**: cite a dictionary, a linguistic survey, or at least two independent published sources per word; mark anything weaker as "to be confirmed by community" and plan it as the "correct us" beat.
3. **No ranking of languages, no mocking of accents, no Hindi-vs-X framing, no "oldest language" claims.** Northeast included where the map appears.
4. **Replay mechanisms only when they improve the story.** State clearly when a blueprint has no replay device by design (Naam reels, by rule).
5. **Writing**: plain, direct English; no em dashes; no hype words (delve, leverage, robust, elevate, tapestry, vibrant, game changer); no "here's the thing"; no "wait for it" or "watch till the end" ever; Hindi in Roman script on screen except when the script itself is the point. CTAs are natural and specific, never "like, share and follow".
6. Length target per blueprint: 900 to 1,400 words. Keep tables tight.

## Blueprint format (all seven parts, in this order, with these headings)

```
# B<nn>. <Reel title>   (series: <series name> · length: <nn> s · replay device: <device or none>)

## 1. Concept
Central idea · Audience (age band, language, region) · Why it matters · Psychological mechanism (cite 01 by section) · Why someone watches

## 2. Opening (0.0 to 3.0 s)
Exact first frame (describe what is on screen, where, size) · First visual · First spoken sentence · Opening text · Sound at 0.0 s · Camera · Movement · Curiosity gap · Why they stop

## 3. Story architecture
Table: Time | Visual | Shot/framing | Camera move | Subject move | VO | On-screen text | SFX | Music | Transition | Emotional purpose | Information revealed
(one row per beat, 1 to 4 s each, every second accounted for)

## 4. Replay architecture
Why rewatch · Hidden detail (what, where, size, when; or "none by design") · Double meaning · Does the ending transform the opening · Visual callback · Audio connection · Is the reveal worth revisiting · What exactly they notice on viewing two

## 5. Engagement architecture
Why share · Who they send it to (the sentence they type) · Why save · What they comment · Why follow · The natural CTA (exact words, VO or on screen)

## 6. Production
Editing instructions · Shot references (patterns, not named creators) · Motion graphics (which of the three signatures, Remotion components to build) · Typography (sizes per 07) · Colour (from 09) · Transitions · Sound design (foley kit names, levels) · Music direction · B-roll (free-licence search queries, e.g. "site:pexels.com Chennai street market") · AI generation (only if needed, and what; no faces) · Export (per 07)

## 7. Performance hypothesis
Primary metric · Secondary metric · Biggest creative risk · A/B test (one variable, what to measure, decision rule) · Replay Scorecard self-score (ten dimensions, weighted total, pass/fail against 70 and the floors)

## Claim list
Table: # | Claim as spoken or shown | Source (title, URL) | Confidence grade | Notes
```
