# Module 11: Creative quality-control gate

Project REPLAY. Written 2026-11-06. Pairs with Module 7 (production bible) and the Replay Scorecard.

## How the two gates fit together

| Gate | When | Tool | Who passes it | Output |
|------|------|------|---------------|--------|
| Concept gate | After the timeline template (Module 7, 7.2) is filled, before any asset is made | The Replay Scorecard (ten dimensions: opening attraction, curiosity, narrative momentum, emotional investment, visual richness, reveal quality, replay motivation, loop quality, shareability, follow potential) scored on the storyboard and VO script | The concept, the timeline, the script | Go, rewrite, or kill |
| Cut gate | On the rendered MP4, before publishing | This checklist (12 criteria out of 100) plus the 20-item technical pre-flight | The finished reel | Ship, fix, or recut |

The concept gate asks "should this exist". The cut gate asks "is this the reel we approved, made well". The cut gate's first ten criteria are the scorecard's ten dimensions, now judged on the real cut, so a reel that scored well at concept and badly at cut tells you the loss happened in production. Criteria 11 and 12 (truth and sound) are craft criteria that only the finished cut can show.

## The 12 questions, expanded into a scored checklist out of 100

Scoring: each criterion gets 0 to 10 against its rubric. Weighted score = rubric score x weight / 10. Total out of 100.

Weights: the two things a reel cannot survive without are an opening that stops the thumb and a reason to watch again, so they carry 12 each. Craft (visual richness) and reveal carry 10 each because they are where a page earns "premium" and where most low-effort reels die. Truth carries 8 because one wrong number costs more than one dull frame. Follow potential carries 4 because it is mostly a function of the other eleven.

| # | Question | Weight | 0 to 2 | 3 to 5 | 6 to 7 | 8 to 10 |
|---|----------|--------|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| 1 | Opening attraction. Does the first second stop the thumb, and does the first frame work as a cover? | 12 | Black or empty first frame, logo sting, or text that needs 2 s to read | A clear subject by 1.0 s but nothing in motion or no question posed | A clear subject, one primary motion and a readable line inside 1.0 s | All of that plus the first frame is already a reframeable image that the ending will change |
| 2 | Curiosity. By 3 s, does the viewer have a question they cannot answer without staying? | 8 | The reel explains before it asks | A question exists but the VO answers it inside 5 s | A real gap, held until at least the midpoint | The gap is specific, the viewer could say it in one sentence, and the reel delays it to the reveal |
| 3 | Narrative momentum. Does every beat change something (a fact, a state, a stake)? | 8 | Two or more beats repeat or restate | One beat is filler; the cut rhythm is flat (all shots the same length) | Every beat moves; rhythm has short runs and long holds | Momentum rises to the turn, drops into the silence, lands on the reveal, and the reframe closes it |
| 4 | Emotional investment. Does the viewer care about a person, a consequence or a stake, not just a number? | 8 | Numbers without consequence | A consequence is stated but shown after the number is forgotten | The human consequence follows the number in the next shot (Kuleshov placement) | The viewer can name who is affected and how, and the grade and sound agree with that feeling |
| 5 | Visual richness and premium craft. Would a motion designer say a person made this? | 10 | Template fingerprints: bouncy easing, glow, shake, default font, fades everywhere | Clean but generic; fewer than three planes; type at or near the floor | House easing, three planes, one camera language, a named grade recipe, one match cut | All of that plus one moment that could not come from a template: a shape match, a masked wipe that uses the scene, a detail that rewards a pause |
| 6 | Reveal quality. Is there a reveal, does it land on one frame, and is it earned by a setup? | 10 | No reveal, or the reveal is a caption | A reveal exists but was answered earlier, or lands over 4 or more frames | One frame, set up 2 to 6 s earlier, with the 0.5 to 1.0 s silence before it | All of that and the reveal re-labels an earlier frame (double layer) |
| 7 | Replay motivation. Is there a genuine reason to watch again that was built into the edit? | 12 | None, or only a caption that says "watch again" | A hidden detail exists but is below spec (too small, too faint, under 24 frames) | A hidden detail at spec, or a double layer, or a callback, and a second viewer finds it unprompted on the second pass | Two devices that work together (for example a planted detail that the ending reframe explains), and a test viewer rewatched without being asked |
| 8 | Loop quality. Does the last frame hand off to the first without a seam? | 8 | Fade to black, end card that holds, music cadence, VO sign-off | A soft loop: same mood, visible jump | Frame-matched or sentence bridge within tolerance (2 px, 1 percent scale, 2 percent luminance; music on the same bar) | Seamless, and the loop frame is the moment the viewer now reads differently |
| 9 | Shareability. Can a viewer say in one sentence why they sent it, and is that sentence in the reel? | 6 | No sendable line | A sendable fact exists but is not on screen | A single sendable line is on screen with its source | The line is on screen, it is the reframe, and it survives the grid crop |
| 10 | Follow potential. Does the reel show that the page has more of this, and is the end card tied to the reel's goal? | 4 | No signal of a series or a page identity | A generic "follow for more" | The end card matches the goal (follow, save, share, comment) and the page look is consistent with the last three reels | All of that and the reel references or sets up the next one |
| 11 | Truth and sources. Is every number sourced on screen, every estimate marked, and nothing invented? | 8 | Any invented or unverifiable figure, any unlicensed asset, any real person's likeness | Sourced in the caption only; an estimate not marked | Every number has a source chip in frame at or above 40 px; estimates dashed and marked | All of that, plus the claim registry is attached with URLs and access dates and the licence log is complete |
| 12 | Sound design and mix. Is sound half the reel? | 6 | Music only, or a mix that peaks over minus 1 dBTP or lands outside minus 14 plus or minus 1 LUFS | VO and music with no foley, no bridges, no planned silence | Foley on primary motions, J and L cuts, one silence, within spec | All of that, an audio motif that returns changed, and every element survives the 150 to 600 Hz phone check |

### Minimum approval and hard floors

| Rule | Value |
|------|-------|
| Minimum total to ship | 75 out of 100 |
| Hard floor: replay motivation (7) | 6 or higher, or the reel does not ship regardless of total |
| Hard floor: opening attraction (1) | 7 or higher |
| Hard floor: truth and sources (11) | 8 or higher. One unsourced number is a fail. |
| Hard floor: visual richness (5) | 6 or higher. A template fingerprint is a fail. |
| Hard floor: loop quality (8) | 5 or higher |
| Any criterion at 0 to 2 | Automatic recut of that element, whatever the total |
| Technical pre-flight | All 20 items pass; any fail blocks publishing |

A reel at 75 with every floor met ships. A reel at 90 with replay at 5 does not.

## Rewrite protocol

When a reel fails, fix in this order. Each step is cheaper than the one after it, and each one can lift several criteria at once.

| Order | Failure | What to rewrite first | Why first |
|-------|---------|------------------------|-----------|
| 1 | Truth (11) below 8 | Remove or source the claim. If the claim was the reveal, go back to the concept gate | A wrong fact invalidates every other fix |
| 2 | Replay motivation (7) below 6 | The timeline's plant, turn and reframe rows. Add or enlarge the hidden detail to spec; make the ending reframe the opening | Replay is the project's point; it is usually a timeline problem, not a render problem |
| 3 | Opening (1) below 7 | The first 30 frames only: first frame as cover, one primary motion, the readable line. Do not touch the rest | The cheapest 1 s to re-render and the most valuable |
| 4 | Reveal (6) below 6 | The 2 s either side of the reveal: add the silence, cut the reveal to one frame, move the setup earlier | Small window, large lift |
| 5 | Loop (8) below 5 | The last 2 s: frame-match to frame 1, cut the end-card hold, put the CTA over the loop frame | Mechanical fix, rarely touches the story |
| 6 | Visual richness (5) below 6 | Replace the offending element (easing curve, glow, fade, font), then add planes and one match cut | Fingerprints are findable with the lint; fix them before adding craft |
| 7 | Sound (12) below 6 | Re-mix: VO lead, foley on primary motions, bridges, silence, loudness | Audio fixes do not require a picture re-render |
| 8 | Momentum, curiosity, emotion (2, 3, 4) | The VO script and the cut rhythm. If two of the three are below 5, return to the concept gate | These are story problems; polishing the render will not fix them |
| 9 | Shareability, follow (9, 10) | The on-screen sendable line and the end card | Last, because they follow from everything above |

Rule: never fix a story failure with a visual effect, and never fix a visual failure with a caption.

## 20-item technical pre-flight

Run on the exported MP4 on a phone at 40 percent brightness, with the phone speaker, inside the Instagram app preview if possible. Every item is pass or fail.

| # | Check | Pass condition |
|---|-------|----------------|
| 1 | Resolution and frame rate | 1080 x 1920, 30.000 fps constant, progressive, square pixels |
| 2 | Codec and container | MP4, H.264 High, 4:2:0 8-bit, AAC-LC 256 kbps 48 kHz stereo |
| 3 | Bitrate and size | 12 to 16 Mbps VBR, file under 4 GB, under the duration limit for the placement |
| 4 | Safe zones | No essential text or number in the top 250 px, bottom 450 px or right 130 px; ad-safe (top 269, bottom 672, sides 65) if the reel may be boosted |
| 5 | Type floor | No text under 40 px; captions at 56 px; hero and headline per the table in Module 7, 3.1 |
| 6 | Contrast | All text at or above 4.5:1; captions at or above 7:1; checked with a contrast tool on the exported frame, not the design file |
| 7 | Hold times | Every on-screen text held at or above the formula (0.6 + 0.35 x words, min 1.0 s); new text at or under 15 characters per second |
| 8 | First frame | Frame 0 is a readable cover candidate: one subject, one line, no black, inside the centre 1080 x 1080 |
| 9 | Last frame and loop | Last frame matches frame 0 within tolerance, or the sentence bridge is clean; no black tail; music on the same bar at both ends |
| 10 | Captions | Burned-in and SRT present, matching, chunked at phrase boundaries, max 2 lines of 32 characters, numbers with units, same as the graphics |
| 11 | Sources on screen | Every number has a source chip in the same frame; every estimate is dashed and marked "est." |
| 12 | Licence log | LICENSES.md lists every stock asset, music cue, font, logo and image with URL, licence, date; nothing from news agencies, no film clips, no real-person likeness without licence |
| 13 | Loudness | Integrated minus 14 LUFS plus or minus 1; true peak at or under minus 1.0 dBTP; LRA 6 to 11 |
| 14 | Phone speaker | Every VO word, impact and foley hit audible on the phone speaker; a 150 to 600 Hz band solo still carries all of them |
| 15 | Mono sum | Nothing disappears when summed to mono |
| 16 | Impact density | 3 or fewer impacts per 30 s, 5 or fewer per reel, none within 4 s of another |
| 17 | Banding and blacks | No visible banding on dark gradients at phone brightness; no large area below 8 percent luminance; grain present on any wide gradient |
| 18 | Fingerprint lint | Brand lint passes: no bounce or elastic easing, no glow, no shake, no particles, no fade used as a crutch, no retired looks |
| 19 | Continuity | Screen direction, timeline direction and A-left B-right comparisons hold for the whole reel; no 180 violations in footage or graphics |
| 20 | Hidden detail spec | The planted detail is at or above 48 px (text) or 80 px (object), 3:1 to 4.5:1 contrast, resolved 3 frames before any primary motion, on screen 24 frames or more, not in the bottom 450 px |

## Notes on use

- Score with two people when possible: the editor and one person who has not seen the reel. The second person's first-watch reactions set criteria 1, 2, 7 and 9; the editor sets 5, 8, 11 and 12.
- The second viewer test for replay (criterion 7) is the only test that counts: play it once, ask what they noticed, tell them the detail, play it again. If they do not find it on the second pass, the detail is below spec.
- Keep the filled sheet in the project folder next to the timeline so the concept-gate scorecard and the cut-gate sheet can be compared. A gap of more than 15 points between the two is a production problem to name in the project report.
- The weights and floors are a starting point. Revisit them after the first 20 reels against the page's own retention and replay data, and record any change in MEMORY.md.
